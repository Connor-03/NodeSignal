NodeSignal — Tor Setup Guide
=======================================================================

Tor is the most private way to run NodeSignal. It needs no port
forwarding, and it does not publish the link between your identity and
your node's IP address — which is the single biggest privacy weakness
of running this over clearnet.

There are TWO halves, and you need both:

    INBOUND   a hidden service, so other operators can reach you
    OUTBOUND  Tor's SOCKS proxy, so you can reach THEIR .onion

Most guides only cover the first. Without the second, your messages to
other operators fail: a .onion address cannot be resolved by DNS, so a
direct connection returns "ENOTFOUND". NodeSignal routes .onion traffic
through the SOCKS proxy automatically once you tell it where that is.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. INSTALL TOR
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Linux:

    sudo apt update && sudo apt install -y tor

Windows:
    Install the Tor Expert Bundle, or Tor Browser (which runs a SOCKS
    proxy on 127.0.0.1:9150 while it is open).


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. INBOUND — publish a hidden service
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Edit your torrc:

    Linux:    /etc/tor/torrc
    Windows:  the torrc in your Tor data directory

Add:

    HiddenServiceDir /var/lib/tor/nodesignal/
    HiddenServicePort 8788 127.0.0.1:8788
    HiddenServicePort 8789 127.0.0.1:8789

Port 8788 is the one other operators need. 8789 is your web interface —
only publish it if you want to reach your own console remotely over Tor,
and if you do, set a login token first.

Restart Tor:

    sudo systemctl restart tor

Read your address:

    sudo cat /var/lib/tor/nodesignal/hostname

That is the address you give to other operators. It looks like:

    abcdefghijklmnop...xyz.onion


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. BIND NODESIGNAL TO LOCALHOST
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Tor connects to the daemon over localhost, so the daemon must NOT be
exposed elsewhere. In nodesignal-config.json:

    "bind": "127.0.0.1"

or on the command line:

    --bind 127.0.0.1

The Windows installer sets this for you if you choose Tor.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. OUTBOUND — point NodeSignal at Tor's SOCKS proxy
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the half that is usually missed. Add:

    "tor-proxy": "127.0.0.1:9050"

or:

    --tor-proxy 127.0.0.1:9050

Which port:

    9050   the tor service (Linux, Tor Expert Bundle)
    9150   Tor Browser (only while the browser is open)

Any address ending in .onion is then dialled through Tor automatically.
Everything else still connects directly.

To force ALL outbound traffic through Tor, including connections to
plain IP addresses, add:

    --tor-all

That is the stronger privacy setting: without it, connecting to a
clearnet peer still reveals your IP to that peer.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ADD A PEER
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Use their .onion address exactly as they gave it to you, in the "add a
contact" dialog. The port stays 8788.

Delivery is slower than clearnet — a Tor circuit adds latency, and the
first connection to a new .onion can take several seconds. A message
that eventually reports "delivered" has worked.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
IF IT DOES NOT WORK
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
"no SOCKS proxy at 127.0.0.1:9050 — is Tor running?"
    Tor is not running, or listens elsewhere. Check:
        sudo systemctl status tor
    If you are using Tor Browser, the port is 9150 and the browser must
    be open.

"Tor could not reach <address> (host unreachable)"
    The .onion is wrong, or that hidden service is down. Onion addresses
    are long and unforgiving — copy and paste, never retype.

"ENOTFOUND ... .onion"
    tor-proxy is not configured, so NodeSignal tried a direct DNS lookup.
    Add the tor-proxy setting from step 4 and restart.

Peers cannot reach YOU
    Check the hidden service is published:
        sudo cat /var/lib/tor/nodesignal/hostname
    and that the daemon is actually listening on 127.0.0.1:8788. The
    HiddenServicePort target must match the daemon's peer port exactly.

Identification shows "not identified"
    Identifying a peer means connecting to its port 8333. Many operators
    do not expose 8333 over their hidden service, so this stays blank
    over Tor. Messaging is unaffected — the two are separate layers.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTES
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
  · No port forwarding is required, on either side.
  · Your .onion address IS your contact address. Share it the way you
    would share a phone number, and remember that doing so links that
    address to whatever identity you share it under.
  · Tor hides your IP from peers. It does not hide the fact that you run
    NodeSignal from anyone watching your own machine.
  · Onion addresses are self-authenticating at the Tor layer, and
    NodeSignal adds its own encrypted handshake with key pinning on top.
