NodeSignal — Security
=======================================================================

This is the practical guide. For the full adversarial threat model —
including what is still weak and why — read SECURITY-CRITIQUE.md.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT NODESIGNAL DOES AND DOES NOT DO
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
It does NOT modify Bitcoin in any way.
It does NOT relay anything through the Bitcoin P2P network.
It does NOT create, sign or broadcast transactions.
It does NOT touch your wallet.

It reads exactly three RPC methods:

    getpeerinfo        who your node is connected to
    getnetworkinfo     your node's version and connection count
    getblockchaininfo  chain and block height

Messages travel directly between operators' daemons on port 8788, on a
channel that has nothing to do with Bitcoin. The Bitcoin protocol has no
message type that can carry chat, and pretending otherwise would get
your node disconnected by its peers.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
ENCRYPTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Every connection runs a Noise-XX handshake over X25519, with
HKDF-SHA256 and ChaCha20-Poly1305.

  · Each daemon has a persistent identity keypair, created on first run
    and stored in state.json. Its fingerprint appears in the boot log
    and in the web interface Status panel.
  · Each session mixes in fresh ephemeral keys, so stealing an identity
    key later cannot decrypt traffic already captured (forward secrecy).
  · Both sides prove they hold their identity key, so a man in the
    middle is detected rather than silently trusted.
  · The first time you talk to someone, their fingerprint is pinned. If
    it ever changes, the connection is REJECTED and the interface shows
    a red banner.

There is no PIN any more. PINs were guessable and shared a fixed salt.

If a peer legitimately reinstalls, their key changes and you will see a
mismatch. Confirm out-of-band that it really was a reinstall, then
remove and re-add the contact to pin the new key.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
PROTECT YOUR RPC CREDENTIALS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Never expose Bitcoin RPC to the internet. It should listen on localhost
only, which is the default.

rpcuser/rpcpassword grants FULL RPC access, including wallet commands.
NodeSignal only needs three read methods, so restrict it in bitcoin.conf:

    rpcwhitelist=nodesignal:getpeerinfo,getnetworkinfo,getblockchaininfo

Prefer the cookie file over a static password where you can, and run the
daemon as an unprivileged user.

The Windows installer stores credentials in nodesignal-config.json and
locks that file to your user account. Do not put passwords on a command
line: on Linux they show up in `systemctl cat`, and on Windows in a
process listing.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
NETWORKING, FROM MOST TO LEAST PRIVATE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Tor           Recommended. No port forwarding, and your node's IP is
                never revealed to peers. See TorSetupGuide.txt. Set
                tor-proxy for outbound as well as a hidden service for
                inbound — you need both halves.

  Tailscale     Good. Private to your tailnet, no ports open to the
                internet. Detected and used automatically.

  Localhost     Fine for testing; nobody else can reach you.

  Clearnet      Works, but publishes the link between your identity and
                your node's IP. Anyone scanning the internet for port
                8788 can enumerate NodeSignal users. Only open the ports
                you actually need, and read SECURITY-CRITIQUE.md first.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS STILL WEAK
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Stated plainly, because you should know before trusting it:

  · Messages are stored DECRYPTED at rest, in state.json. Anyone with
    filesystem access — a backup, a snapshot, a stolen disk — can read
    your history.
  · Running NodeSignal links a social identity to a node IP. That is a
    property of the design, not a bug, and it is the reason to prefer
    Tor.
  · Content is encrypted; metadata is not. Who you talk to, when, and
    how often is visible to anyone positioned to watch.
  · Every identity claim a peer makes about its node is self-declared.
    The 8333 handshake proves something at that address speaks the
    Bitcoin protocol and claims a given user agent. Nothing more.

Do not run this on a node holding significant value without reading
SECURITY-CRITIQUE.md in full.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
KEEP THINGS UPDATED
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Bitcoin Core / Knots, Node.js, and NodeSignal itself.

NodeSignal has no third-party dependencies, so there is no npm supply
chain to audit — it uses only Node's standard library.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
IF YOU SUSPECT A PROBLEM
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Red banner about an identity mismatch
    Someone at that address presented a different key than the one you
    pinned. Do not dismiss it. Confirm with that operator through
    another channel before re-adding them.

Unexpected contacts appearing
    Anyone who completes a handshake with your daemon becomes a contact.
    Remove them, and consider moving to Tor or Tailscale so strangers
    cannot reach your peer port at all.

Suspected compromise
    Stop the daemon, move state.json aside (it holds your identity key
    and message history), and restart to generate a fresh identity. Tell
    your contacts, since they will see a fingerprint mismatch.
